linkedin: |
  When you connect an AI agent to your accounts today, you hand it an API key or an OAuth token. The service on the other end validates the token, not the intent behind it. It cannot tell you from your agent doing what you asked, or from an attacker who read the key out of an environment file.

  MOI Network moves the authority out of the token entirely. It is the Participant Layer: a base protocol where you exist natively in computation. Your state lives on your own record, and authority over an agent is a live relationship you hold, never a credential the agent carries. Tokens witness that relationship. They do not contain it.

  That changes revocation. Cancel a grant and it is one state advance on your own record, final the instant you issue it. No revocation list, no TTL, no cache to wait out.

  The post walks the whole chain: the paradigm underneath, the four classes of value, the Context Superstate that holds a participant's state, and MOI Agent Launchpad, the reference application. MOI is live on DevNet now.
